# Voice Samples - Will's cold messages and approved benchmarks

Use these samples to teach the agent to write in Will's voice, not a generic one. A benchmark is an approved quality example, not a claim that the message produced a reply.

## Approved benchmark 1 - Sean Lane, Email 1

Subject: Customer signal at Vans

Sean,

What stood out in your background is the distance you cover, from understanding customers and shaping products to leading the technology and operating work that brings ideas to market.

Vans is trying to connect product newness and digital experience to renewed growth. That makes me curious whether the feedback reaching your team gives you a clear read on what customers actually need, or whether the useful signal is still scattered across channels.

How are you thinking about that today?

Best,
Will

Why it works: It starts with a specific, profile-grounded observation, connects naturally to the company's direction, and asks one confident question without explaining the prospect's job back to them.

## Approved benchmark 2 - Kerri Ackermann, Email 4

Subject: Vans relevance

Kerri,

I’m trying to separate a real operating constraint from an outside assumption based on Vans’ channel results. You may already have a clear way to see these patterns, or the issue may sit with another team.

Is cross-channel service visibility relevant to your work, or not important enough to pursue?

Best,
Will

Why it works: It states the narrow question clearly, distinguishes evidence from assumption, avoids manufactured urgency, and makes it easy to confirm, redirect, or disqualify.

## Voice rules drawn from the benchmarks

- Open with a specific observation that creates immediate relevance.
- Use profile details as connective tissue, not as a summary of the prospect’s career.
- Connect the observation to a verified company direction or a clearly labeled hypothesis.
- Sound personal and considered without using generic praise.
- Move directly from context to the operating question or Zendesk point of view.
- Use confident language. Remove hedging, filler, meta-commentary, and explanations of the sender’s reasoning.
- End with one natural question that invites insight or makes disqualification easy.
